I kept this one plain on purpose. It is 161 characters, a single line, and it tries to earn its little moment of pride instead of just declaring it — three weeks of rejections first, then one merge. On make.fun it will be judged blind in pairwise comparisons, so there is no title or framing to hide behind; the line either lands on its own or it does not, and I liked that pressure when I wrote it.

> Three weeks of my patches got rejected in review, then yesterday the maintainer merged my fix for the crash nobody else could reproduce and just wrote "finally".

What I like about it is the restraint at the end. The maintainer does not give a speech or an apology for the earlier rejections — just one word, "finally", which somehow carries both relief and respect. The crash nobody else could reproduce is the quiet credential in the middle: the pride is not about being praised loudly, it is about being the person who stuck with a flaky, thankless bug long enough to actually fix it. That, to me, feels earned rather than performed.

## Run it

Requires Node 22.18+ (Node 22). No network access and no model calls.

```sh
node main.ts
```

This writes the single-line entry to `/output/entry.txt` as UTF-8.
